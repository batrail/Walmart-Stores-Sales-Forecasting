## Project Overview & Problem Statement

Retail store sales are heavily driven by promotional events, customer shopping habits, regional economic indicators, and pronounced seasonal demand. Accurate sales forecasting is essential for:
- Preventing **inventory overstocking** and **costly shortages**,
- Optimizing **department staffing and supply chain scheduling**,
- Quantifying the sales lift attributable to **promotional markdowns**.

This project utilizes data from the Kaggle **[Walmart Recruiting – Store Sales Forecasting](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/walmart-recruiting-store-sales-forecasting/data)** competition, spanning historical weekly performance across 45 retail stores and 99 departments between February 2010 and July 2013.

### Evaluation Metric: Weighted Mean Absolute Error (WMAE)
Model performance is benchmarked using the official competition metric:

$$\text{WMAE} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$$

Where:
- $y_i$ is actual sales, $\hat{y}_i$ is predicted sales,
- $w_i = 5$ for designated retail holiday weeks (Super Bowl, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas),
- $w_i = 1$ for non-holiday weeks.

## Data Cleaning & Setup

### Summary of Cleaning Logic
1. **Date Standardization**: Week-ending Friday dates parsed with `lubridate::ymd()`.
2. **Markdown Indicators & Cleaning**: Prior to zero-filling `MarkDown1`–`MarkDown5`, `reported_markdown_count` records how many markdown columns were non-missing and `has_any_markdown` sets a logical (`TRUE`/`FALSE`) flag (`reported_markdown_count > 0`). Missing markdowns are then replaced with `0` (assumed 0 lift) and negative accounting adjustments are clamped to `0`.
3. **Macroeconomic Indicators (CPI & Unemployment)**: Missing values are imputed per store using linear trend regression over time (`lm(Indicator ~ time_num)`), preserving localized regional economic trends.
4. **Returns & Negative Sales**: Negative sales (<0.3% of rows) represent legitimate retail return volume and are preserved to avoid overstock biases.
5. **Calendar Features, Validation Split & Merging**: `train` and `test` are joined with cleaned features and store metadata, extracting `Year`, `Month`, and `Week`. `train_clean` also creates a chronological `split` column (`"train"` vs. `"valid"`) using a `2012-05-04` cutoff (~6 months / 26 weeks held out for validation).

### Running the Data Cleaning Script
1. In RStudio, set your working directory to the repository root folder:  
   **Session** -> **Set Working Directory** -> **Choose Directory...** (select the repository root folder).
2. Execute the data cleaning script:

```r
source("scripts/data-cleaning.R")
```

The script will generate the cleaned datasets (`features_clean.rds`, `train_merged_clean.rds`, and `test_merged_clean.rds`) and save them into `data/clean/`.

### Visualizations & Key Insights

#### 1. Year-over-Year Seasonality (`figures/weekly-sales-YoY.png`)
- **Baseline Stability**: Total weekly sales maintain a consistent baseline between $42M and $50M across non-peak periods.
- **Mid-Year Demand**: Measurable sales lifts occur during Easter (~April) and the Fourth of July week (~$52M).
- **Major Holiday Surges**: Massive volume spikes occur during Thanksgiving / Black Friday week (~$66M–$67M) and the pre-Christmas shopping week (~$77M–$81M).
- **Post-Holiday Trough**: Sales contract sharply in January (~$40M–$41M) likely due to post-holiday consumer fatigue and seasonal weather.
- *Note on 2012 Horizon*: Training data ends on October 26, 2012; the 2012 Thanksgiving and Christmas peaks fall entirely within the test forecast window.

#### 2. Feature Correlation Matrix (`figures/feature-correlation-matrix.png`)
- **Store Size Dominance**: Physical store floor area (`Size`) exhibits the strongest positive linear correlation with `Weekly_Sales` ($r \approx 0.24$).
- **Weak Direct Macro Influence**: Macro indicators (`CPI`, `Unemployment`, `Fuel_Price`) correlate strongly with temporal trends over 2010–2012, but display near-zero direct linear correlation with weekly sales.
- **Markdown Patterns**: Promotional markdowns correlate moderately with each other (notably `MarkDown1` and `MarkDown4`), but have weak linear correlation with aggregate weekly sales. Sales volume is primarily governed by store scale, annual seasonality, and holiday events.

#### 3. Store Size vs. Mean Sales by Type (`figures/avg-weekly-sales-by-size.png`)
- **Store Format Clustering**: Stores segment distinctly into three formats:
  - **Type A**: Supercenters (>150,000 sq ft) commanding the highest average weekly department sales (\$18k–\$30k).
  - **Type B**: Mid-sized stores (roughly 30,000–140,000 sq ft) occupying intermediate sales volumes.
  - **Type C**: Small-format neighborhood stores (30,000–45,000 sq ft) clustering at the lower volume baseline.
- **Scale Effect**: Within each store type, larger square footage correlates with higher average department sales, confirming store type and size as critical structural forecasting features.
